A little introduction about myself: I was born in Italy in the 80s and when I was around thirteen years old my parents enrolled me for some IT courses. At that time I started with BASIC, to then pass to DBIII Plus, then C, to end with pc building course. 

At that time internet was not a thing yet, I had to wait for few more years before I could have my first 56k modem at home, so the only way to learn some IT skills was with someone that could pass them on or books. 

Most of the books were in English, and unfortunately, despite the fact English was a subject in school, it was not to a level that would allow to approach something technical so easy. Few years forward, I joined my family business in hospitality after a short parenthesis in uni, and despite my passion for IT, I got comfortable and I did not cultivate this passion. 

## Moving in the UK
Jumping few more years, in 2014 I moved in the UK, and in 2015 I was reach by my wife and children. One of the few thing I was able to do is work in hospitality, so is were in ended up working. 

Thank to my sweet half, that kept pushing me, in 2018 I decided to go back to uni while working full time. I enrolled in a BSc in IT management for business at the University of West London. 

There my passion for tech and IT flourish again, taking me back at my teenage years. During my course I was able to touch programming language like python, JavaScript and java,  unfortunately not to a level deep enough that would make me feel confident to apply for a job. And there is where I decided to specialize further with a MSc in cybersecurity at the University of Hertfordshire.

## The evolution of learning
During my bachelor I practically started from zero again with python, yes my previous study in old programming language helped me in reasoning and logic, I could still create some pseudo-code, but in more than 20 years the leap was huge. 

During the various assignment we were tasked to do a lot of programming exercises, and not always things would work the way I wanted. In those days Google, and more specifically stack overflow, were my best friend. 

I have fond memory of that period: every time I would get stuck on something the hunt begun. I was stubborn to overcome the obstacle, and I remember getting lost in google search and countless of stack overflow post, until I would be able to find a solution to my problem. 

Sometimes would have been a matter of minutes, sometimes could have taken an hour or more. But man, I was so razor focused that nothing around me would matter. Time would pass without me even realizing, to then realize that what should have been a 30 minutes study session would soon become 3-4 hours. 

My feelings would pass from confusion to frustration, but the fulfilling satisfaction once you make things works was priceless, and all that time spent was worth it!!! Sure, sometimes would become so late (always used to study at evening/night) that I had to give up, but that would not mean that during the next day I would not think about that, to then go back as soon as possible. 

### The advent of AI
In 2022 we had ChatGPT first release, a big leap in technology, and from there so many other generative AI and apps that we can loose counts of. I would says that early models were gimmicky, more of a sort of a big encyclopaedia, but the information available were dated to a couple of year earlier. 

Then, a new model came out, and a new one, starting to having access to internet, more features that most of us don't even know they exist. That was a second leap, and I think that I can speak for many of us, sometimes when I need to search for something I just skip google and use one of the generative AI at my disposal. 

In my opinion AI has a big merit: is making tech and IT more approachable to those people that not know nothing about it, and boosted the efficiency of those already in the industry. But what about learning?

Generative AI is a great learning tool. You can easily research anything you want, ask to guide you with step by step process. Indeed, in my guide to [[Building an Automated Cybersecurity Blog with Obsidian, Quartz, and GitHub Pages]] is what i did. And done in many other occasion!

AI allowed me to do more in less time, but, the other side of the medal is that sometimes I feel like more an automata. Almost heedlessly following instruction, no more hunt, no more discovery and more importantly no more fulfilling satisfaction.  AI made me lazy. 

Luckily, AI is not always to be able to solve all the issue. Building this blog, in the left bar, under the Fresh Release, it would show not only the latest post, but also the list of the latest tags. I asked few times how to modify the ```quartz.config.yaml``` file, but with no success. 

And then I remembered. The "old" way. I went to quartz website, documentation. Yes, documentation, this underrated document that if done well can hold all the answers to our question. And there it was, what i was looking for `hideTagPages: true`. And boom, that feeling came back again!!!

## Where I am now
If you followed through you may think that I would say, or suggest, abandon AI and go back to the old school method. But not. In a world that is running so fast, If we want to keep the pace, is not possible to ignore AI. 

Working full time not in the IT industry leave me little time to progress on my end goal to get a job in IT, so AI is something that definitely I will keep leveraging. But sometimes, I want to go back to the hunt. The struggle is what carved the little knowledge I have so far, because when you put effort to reach something, you give it more value and make treasure of it.
